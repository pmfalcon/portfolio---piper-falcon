Project overview

Verdant Echoes explores how emotional cues can be communicated through environmental design. I modeled and developed emotion-based plant and decorative assets, translating abstract ideas into form, color, and material within an immersive space.

01 / Enter the environment

Project overview

Breath Between Spacesis a bio-responsive virutal reality experience designed to help medical students and healthcare professionals reconnect with their bodies through breath, stillness, and immersive environmental interaction. Instead of rewarding constant movement, the experience encourages users to slow down, breathe, and simply be present within a peaceful Zen-inspired landscape.

Concept & intent

The project explores how virtual environments can encourage mindfulness through active presence rather than passive relaxation. Breathing becomes the primary form of interaction, creating a visual bio-feedback loop where the world gently responds to the user's physiological state.

02 / Environment Development

My role

I led concept development and collaborated to create the immersive environment through 3D modeling, Unity scene assembly, environmental composition, and real-time visual effects. My work focused on building a calming atmosphere that supported mindful exploration while maintaining optimized VR performance.

03 / Visual effects & shaders

Technical implementation

The environment was developed in Unity using Shader Graph, particle systems, and real-time visual effects. Flowing water, animated waterfalls, drifting leaves, and subtle environmental movement were designed to reinforce relaxation without distracting from the breathing experience.

04 / Bio-Responsive interaction

The world responds directly to the user's breathing, encouraging slower, more intentional breath patterns. Stillness itself becomes an interaction mechanic-certain experiences only become available once the participant pauses movement and remains present, shifting progression away from speed and toward mindfulness.

01 / Creative Direction

Project Overview

Creative Direction is an ongoing collection of speculative branding projects developed for musicians and bands. Each identity explores how typography, imagery, merchandise, motion, and promotional materials can translate an artist's sound into a cohesive visual language. Rather than redesigning existing branding, these projects imagine new creative campaigns that extend the atmosphere of the music into physical and digital experiences.

02 / Jadu Heart

Rather than reflecting Jadu Heart's existing branding, this project explores an alternate visual identity that pushes further into the band's experimental electronic sound. Influenced by retro-futurism, CRT technology, digital decay, and surreal dreamscapes, the identity uses fragmented imagery, layered textures, and cinematic motion to create a world that feels immersive, mysterious, and emotionally charged. The resulting system spans album artwork, tour posters, merchandise, and animated visuals, imagining how the band's visual language could evolve alongside its more electronic and atmospheric music.

01 / The Concept

Project overview

NeoGen is a real-time interactive installation concept developed as a submission for the Seven Stars Music Festival. Participants' bodies become points of attraction within a living particle system — no controllers, screens, or instructions required. The moment someone steps into the space, their presence alone begins shaping the environment around them.

Concept & intent

Inspired by natural phenomena — the pull of starlight, the drift of energy fields — NeoGen visualizes the invisible forces that draw people together. The piece was designed around four core values: inclusion (no prior knowledge needed), curiosity (every gesture reveals something new), sharing (the work grows richer as more people join), and wild abandon (there is no "correct" way to engage).

02 / From Prototype to Installation

My role

I led concept development, system design, and the working prototype build. Using TouchDesigner and real-time hand tracking as a stand-in for full-body presence detection, I built a system where each hand functions as an attractor node, with particles responding dynamically to proximity and motion. I also authored the concept pitch deck outlining the installation experience, technical approach, and audience interaction design for festival submission.

03 / Technical System

The prototype runs in TouchDesigner, using MediaPipe-based tracking to detect participant position in real time and feed it into a GPU particle system. Particles respond dynamically to proximity and motion, transforming physical gestures into a shifting field of light. The final installation is designed to scale this system to full-body depth sensing, with multiple participants tracked simultaneously across a shared space — no internet connection required, with all processing running locally.

04 / Deployment & Experience Design

As part of the pitch, I designed three deployment paths to fit different festival environments — a floor projection setup (most intuitive, particles emerging from participants' feet), a vertical LED wall (easiest to deploy, most self-contained), and an enclosed canopy or dome structure (most immersive, projecting the particle field overhead). Each configuration was scoped with its own spatial footprint, power, lighting, and budget requirements, ranging from roughly $800 for a simple LED wall setup to $8,000 for a full dome installation — giving festival organizers flexibility depending on venue and budget.

01 / Live Visual Direction

Project overview

This is an ongoing visual and lighting development project for Jordan Rein's band, focused on building a live show language that feels atmospheric, tactile, and organic rather than like a conventional DJ visual package. The system combines projection, custom lighting, live camera imagery, analog-inspired processing, and purpose-built visual assets into a flexible toolkit for performance.

Creative direction

The visual direction centers on imperfect physical textures: water, shadows, reflections, film and VHS artifacts, cracked surfaces, soft light, and slow-moving organic forms. Instead of filling the stage with constant high-energy graphics, the goal is to create moments that breathe with the music and make the performers feel embedded inside the environment.

02 / Lighting language

A small lighting vocabulary keeps the show cohesive: warm white profile lighting, custom E-size gobos, hard-edged shadows, water-like patterns, blinds or golden-hour textures, cracks, ripples, and distorted line work. The approach is intentionally simple and physical, using light as an active material instead of relying on a large number of fixtures.

03 / Live camera + projection

One of the central experiments is projecting live footage from an iPhone or camcorder and deliberately degrading or delaying the image. Camera footage can be processed through TouchDesigner to introduce ghosting, feedback, blur, noise, scan lines, and other analog-looking artifacts before being sent to the projector. This creates a live image that feels closer to a physical video artifact than a polished digital feed.

04 / Analog visual experiments

Additional experiments explore liquid light projection, oil and water movement, textured glass, CRT/video artifacts, and physical materials placed between light and camera. These tests help build a library of source material that can be captured, manipulated, and recombined during a show.

05 / TouchDesigner visual system

TouchDesigner acts as the real-time layer that can combine live camera input, pre-made visual studies, analog-style effects, and reactive processing. A MIDI controller can be used to move between visual states, adjust parameters, and introduce variation without interrupting the performance. The system is designed as a small set of modular tools rather than one fixed video sequence.

06 / Performance system

The developing setup can scale from a lightweight projector-and-camera configuration to a more involved show system with multiple sources. A possible signal path is camera → capture / NDI or wired video input → TouchDesigner → projector, with lighting operating alongside the projection system. The intent is to keep the core setup portable while leaving room for additional layers such as depth-camera interaction, MIDI control, custom gobos, CRT elements, or other physical projection experiments.

Current stage

This project is currently in concept development and technical prototyping. The case study documents the visual research, lighting language, hardware experiments, and real-time system being developed toward a future live performance rather than presenting the work as an already completed tour or show.

My role

Creative direction, live visual concepting, lighting and projection research, TouchDesigner system development, analog visual experimentation, gobo design, technical planning, and development of a modular visual language for live performance.

01 / The Publication

Project overview

Rip Current / 1997 is a fictional editorial publication inspired by the visual language of 1990s surf magazines. Rather than recreating a vintage publication literally, I reinterpreted its tactile photography, imperfect typography, collage-driven layouts, and youth culture through a contemporary art direction.

Concept & intent

The publication treats surfing as a starting point rather than a subject. The issue expands outward into road trips, music, photography, fashion, film, local culture, and the small moments that make a summer feel endless. The goal was to create a magazine that feels discovered rather than designed — something sun-faded, personal, and slightly imperfect.

02 / Visual Language

I developed the visual system around 1990s surf and alternative music culture, combining film photography, photocopy textures, handwritten notes, oversized typography, archival imagery, and layered editorial compositions. The layouts intentionally balance structured grids with moments of visual disorder to create the feeling of a publication assembled from photographs, notes, receipts, and memories.

03 / Road Trip Editorial

The Last Exit Before the Ocean

The road trip feature became an opportunity to explore editorial pacing and visual storytelling. I built the layout around bold type, layered imagery, and a loose grid system to create contrast between structured information and the imperfect energy of a 90s-inspired publication.

04 / Music & Culture

I wanted these pages to feel less like traditional music advertisements and more like visual artifacts from the worlds of the artists themselves. Experimenting with scale, texture, typography, and image treatment allowed me to build distinct visual identities while keeping the layouts connected to the larger editorial language.

05 / Speculative Advertising

Dive Into the Sound

I created a speculative music advertisement for fictional band Sugar Static and their album No One Home. The campaign turns the vinyl record into a surreal swimming pool, transforming the physical format of the music into an environment. The facing page shifts from surreal image-making into a tactile product composition, presenting the record and sleeve as an object found on the beach.

My role

I developed the publication concept, art direction, editorial layouts, typography system, image treatment, advertising concepts, and visual identity. The project allowed me to explore graphic design through the lens of cultural storytelling — treating the magazine not simply as a collection of pages, but as a complete visual world.

Design approach

The final publication combines editorial structure with intentionally imperfect details: layered photographs, handwritten annotations, analog textures, oversized type, and unexpected visual relationships. I wanted the work to feel nostalgic without becoming a direct recreation of the past — using the visual language of the 1990s to tell a new story.

About Piper

Making the digital feel tactile.

I'm an interdisciplinary designer working in the space between physical interaction and digital environments. My practice moves across VR, real-time generative systems, and brand design, because I'm drawn to the same question from every angle: how do you make technology feel felt, not just seen? I work fluently across Blender, Maya, TouchDesigner, and Unity for 3D and real-time systems, and Adobe's creative suite, Figma, and front-end code (HTML/CSS/JS) for everything visual and web-facing. But the tools are secondary. What I truly care about is atmosphere: building things that ask people to slow down, pay attention, and notice their own presence inside a space. I'm currently based in Richmond, VA, graduating from VCU in a program I built myself — Creative Technological Practices — because no single major covered everything I wanted to make.
